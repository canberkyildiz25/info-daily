Grand Theft Auto VI comes out on 19 November 2026 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. Pre-loading opens a week earlier, at local midnight on 12 November. Rockstar has not announced a PC version.

This is a preparation guide, not a review. Nothing here comes from playing the game. It covers the part you can sort out now, on the console you already own: where the game will go, how long it will take to arrive, and which downloads to ignore.

## The Box Has No Disc in It

This is the detail most people will miss. According to [Rockstar's own support page](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/4QfG4FmZCf5W1gS8jy4UVT/grand-theft-auto-vi-platform-editions-and-versions), the physical edition contains a download code, and "a disc will not be included in the box." Boxed copies reach shops on 12 November so that their owners can pre-load too.

So the boxed copy is no way around the download. Every player installs the full game over the internet, and whether you bought it in a shop or not, you need the free space and the bandwidth.

Two smaller points from the same page:

- **PlayStation codes are tied to a region.** A code bought in one region only works on a PlayStation account registered in the same group of countries. Rockstar recommends buying from a retailer in the country your account belongs to.
- **Xbox codes are not region-locked.**

## How Big Is It? Nobody Outside Rockstar Knows Yet

As of 19 October, Rockstar has not published an install size. The figure will appear on the PlayStation Store and Microsoft Store listings, and we will add it here when it does.

You will see confident numbers online anyway. Treat them as guesses. The "113GB ISO" that circulated in August, for instance, turned out to be malware (more on that below).

The most useful reference point is Rockstar's previous game: for the 2019 PC release of Red Dead Redemption 2, [Rockstar listed 150GB](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/red-dead-redemption-2-pc-specs) of storage. GTA VI is a newer and larger game. **Clearing at least 200GB** gives you a sensible margin until the real number is published. That figure is our planning margin, not an estimate of the game's size.

## How Much Room Your Console Actually Has

A console's advertised capacity is not what you get. The system software takes a share before you install anything:

- **PS5 (original, 2020):** 825GB advertised, about 667GB usable
- **PS5 Slim:** 1TB advertised, about 848GB usable
- **PS5 Pro:** 2TB advertised, about 1.89TB usable
- **Xbox Series X:** 1TB advertised, 802GB usable
- **Xbox Series S:** 512GB advertised, 364GB usable

The PS5 figures come from [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/ps5-usable-storage-confirmed-and-its-much-less-than-825gb), [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/ps5-1tb-usable-storage-answer/) (the number the Slim's storage menu shows) and [PlayStation Universe](https://www.psu.com/news/the-ps5-pro-has-1-89tb-of-usable-storage/). The Xbox figures were [confirmed by GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/xbox-series-s-has-364-gb-usable-space/1100-6484069/) on a review unit.

The tight case is the **512GB Xbox Series S**. With 364GB to work with, one very large game can take more than half the drive. If that is your console, start clearing space this week rather than on the evening of the 11th. The 1TB Series S Microsoft released in 2023 has noticeably more room.

## Making Space Without Losing Anything

You do not have to delete games for good. Both consoles let you park games on a cheap USB drive and bring them back later. That is much faster than downloading them again.

**On PS5**, go to Settings → Storage → Console Storage → Games and Apps to see what is taking the room. A USB drive has to be formatted as USB extended storage first (Settings → Storage), and [Sony's guide](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/support/hardware/ps5-extended-storage/) sets out what it can do: PS4 games play straight from it, but "PlayStation 5 games can't be played directly from USB extended storage". You copy them back when you want them. Check the Media Gallery as well: saved video clips add up quickly.

**On Xbox**, open My games & apps → See all → Manage. Older Xbox One and Xbox 360 games will run from a USB drive. Games built for Series X|S can be stored on one, but [they must be moved back](https://www.seagate.com/products/gaming-drives/xbox-gaming/storage-expansion-for-xbox-series-x/faq/) to the internal drive or an expansion card before you can play them.

If you would rather add space than shuffle it:

- **PS5:** any internal M.2 drive Sony supports. That means PCIe Gen4 x4 NVMe, 250GB to 8TB, with 5,500MB/s or faster recommended and a heatsink required, according to [Sony's requirements](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/support/hardware/ps5-install-m2-ssd/).
- **Xbox:** a Storage Expansion Card, which plugs into the back of the console. It is the only add-on drive that plays Series X|S games directly.

Be aware that SSD prices are well up this year because of the [global memory shortage](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/video-game-hardware-sales-plunge-to-13-year-low-as-prices-soar-on-memory-storage-shortage-155535332.html). Moving a few old games to a USB drive costs nothing.

## Use the Pre-load Week

Pre-loading is the reason to pre-order digitally, or to redeem a boxed code as soon as you have it. The download can then run for a week instead of an evening.

To put numbers on it: a 200GB download on a steady 100Mbps connection takes roughly four and a half hours. At 50Mbps that doubles. Real-world speeds on launch week tend to be lower than the advertised rate, when millions of people are downloading the same thing. If your provider caps monthly data, a download this size can use a large share of the allowance.

Downloads carry on overnight if the console is in rest mode with "Stay Connected to the Internet" switched on (PS5), or in the Sleep power mode (Xbox). Leave some room beyond the game itself as well: Rockstar has not said whether there will be a launch-day update, but large releases often get one.

## Every "PC Download" Is Fake

There is no PC version, so anything offering GTA VI for a computer — a "leaked build", an "ISO", a "crack", an "early access demo" — is fake, and several of them are dangerous.

- In August, a 113GB "ISO" did the rounds. Testers found it was [99.99% empty padding](https://www.techradar.com/pro/of-course-this-fake-gta-vi-iso-download-is-malware-testers-reveal-113gb-download-is-99-99-empty-zeroes-with-a-tiny-virus-attached) wrapped around a small piece of malware.
- In September, the security firm Huntress examined another fake installer. [It bundled](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/10/fake-gta-6-downloads-malware-ransomware/) two remote-access tools, a password and Discord-token stealer, and a variant of Chaos ransomware set up to destroy files rather than ransom them.
- [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/25/that-fake-grand-theft-auto-vi-demo-is-actually-just-malware/) on a fake "demo" that was also just malware.

The rule is simple: the game comes from the PlayStation Store or the Microsoft Store and nowhere else. If someone has sent you a link, that is reason enough not to click it.

## Clear the Space This Week

Nothing about launch day is in your hands except your console's storage. Check how much room you have now, move older games to a USB drive rather than deleting them, and if you are on a 512GB Series S, give yourself more margin than you think you need. Then let the pre-load run from 12 November.
