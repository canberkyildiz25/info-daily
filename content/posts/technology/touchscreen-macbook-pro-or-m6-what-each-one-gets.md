Apple announced two new MacBook Pros within two days. On 27 October came a [14-inch model with the M6 chip](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/27/14-inch-macbook-pro-with-m6-chip/), starting at $1,999. A day later came [the one everyone was waiting for](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/28/apple-new-macbook-pro/): a redesigned MacBook Pro with a touch-sensitive OLED screen, from $2,899 for the 14-inch and $3,399 for the 16-inch.

**We have not used either laptop, and they don't ship until 6 November.** Everything below is what Apple has stated, as reported by MacRumors. Treat performance and display figures as Apple's own claims until independent reviews appear.

## What the $2,899 Model Adds

- **A touch OLED display.** Apple calls it PROLED XDR: tandem OLED, up to 2,000 nits, a 10Hz to 120Hz refresh rate, and a touch sensor built into the panel.
- **A new shape.** Up to 1mm slimmer, about three pounds for the 14-inch and four for the 16-inch. The notch is gone, replaced by a smaller Dynamic Island.
- **Slightly larger screens.** Thinner bezels make them 14.2 and 16.3 inches.
- **A new hinge**, meant to hold steady when you touch the screen, and a six-speaker system.
- **Ports:** three Thunderbolt 5, HDMI, an SD card slot, MagSafe and a headphone jack.

macOS translates touch into clicks, Apple says, so many existing apps will respond to touch without being updated.

## What the $1,999 Model Adds

The M6 model keeps the existing design and display. MacRumors reports that apart from the following, it is unchanged from the M5 version:

- **The M6 chip.** Apple claims up to 30% faster graphics and up to 25% faster AI workloads than M5.
- **Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6.**
- **Dual external 4K displays at up to 165Hz**, up from 144Hz.

It still starts with 1TB of storage.

## The Newer Chip Is Not in the Better Laptop

This is the part that catches people out. The OLED MacBook Pro uses the M5 Pro and M5 Max, the same chips as the models Apple announced in March, with a redesigned cooling layout. It does not use M6.

That doesn't necessarily make it the slower machine: M6 is the base chip in Apple's range, while the Pro and Max are higher tiers. It does mean you cannot have the newest generation and the new screen in one laptop, and you are paying that $900 for the display and the redesign rather than for a newer processor. Apple also continues to sell the existing mini-LED Pro models alongside both.

## What Nobody Has Measured Yet

Three questions decide whether the OLED model is worth the money, and none can be answered from a launch event:

- **Is touch useful on a laptop?** Apple's examples are swiping through a webpage, pinching a photo and dragging slides between presentations. Whether people keep doing that after the first week is a real question.
- **Battery life.** We have not seen independent figures for either model.
- **How well does the cooling hold up?** Apple says the new thermal design directs warm air away from the display. Sustained performance under load is where reviewers will test that.

## If You Already Own a MacBook

Nothing announced in October makes a working Apple silicon MacBook worse. Ask what you actually want to change:

- **If it's the screen,** the OLED model is the only one that offers it. Wait for reviews on 6 November or soon after, since the first batch of orders won't arrive before then anyway.
- **If it's speed on a recent machine,** the gain from one chip generation is modest by Apple's own numbers. Compare your current chip with benchmarks when they are published, rather than the launch percentages.
- **If your machine's real problem is storage or memory,** check what a new configuration costs first. [Memory and SSD prices](/technology/ram-and-ssd-prices-which-pc-upgrades-still-make-sense) are well up this year.

## Reviews First, Unless You Need One Now

If you need a laptop this month, the M6 model at $1,999 is the cheaper and simpler choice. If the touch OLED screen is what tempts you, there is little cost to waiting a week or two to see whether it holds up.
